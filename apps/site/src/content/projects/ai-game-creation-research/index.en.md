## Background

The study examines how university students use professional AI programming tools and all-in-one AI game-creation platforms, and how players evaluate games involving AI. It was narrowed to three paths: Codex-assisted programming, all-in-one creation with TapTap Maker as a case anchor, and evaluation by people who had actually played AI-involved games.

## My Role

I coordinated the 14-person team, aligned research questions and sample definitions, handled the H5 product and backend flow, integrated the report structure, led report writing, and delivered the final presentation. Team members supported surveys, interviews, desk research, data processing, and material preparation. I adjusted priorities as scope and staffing changed and maintained one review process through final delivery.

## Core Questions

- Which tasks do AI programming tools enter, and how are results checked, modified, and integrated into projects?
- How do all-in-one platforms move ideas into testable results, and what work remains after generation?
- How do players understand AI involvement, and how do gameplay, feedback, audiovisual quality, and text affect later choices?

## Process

The study exported 117 questionnaire records. Of these, 115 were usable, 69 university-student samples formed the primary analysis scope, and five interviews were completed. The H5 product handled question routing, profile results, and sharing, while the backend received and organized data. The public case shows only aggregated methods, figures, and findings; it does not publish the live backend, raw answers, transcripts, or sample IDs.

## AI Collaboration

Codex supported H5 and backend development, structure checks, data-processing scripts, and document organization. I defined the research questions and sample rules, verified figures, judged the strength of each conclusion, and took responsibility for the final report. AI participation in the tooling and organization process does not mean the conclusions were automatically generated.

## Outcomes

The team delivered the H5 product and backend, research report, final deck, and presentation. The evidence suggests that AI tools can help users form program or prototype results faster, but requirement breakdown, runtime checks, playtesting, and human selection remain necessary. Player evaluation still centered on the quality of the work.

## Retrospective

The study used a non-probability sample and cannot represent all university students. Platform-creation records came from multiple platforms and do not support TapTap-Maker-only user conclusions. There was also no causal experiment comparing AI labels. These limits define how far the findings can extend while preserving the process observations supported by the material.
