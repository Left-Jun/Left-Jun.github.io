## Background

This individual assignment uses a simulated commercial context rather than a real live-service project. The prompt asks a new PM to understand a six-month-old IP action PVP game and respond to resource, scope, and testing conflicts. I used my action-game research, game-jam work, and independent development experience as category context, then translated that context into an executable takeover and decision process.

## My Role

I independently defined the project frame, designed the 48-hour takeover SOP, answered three conflict scenarios, and produced the launch review and three-month roadmap. AI supported structure, omission checks, and option exploration; I set the constraints, selected the options, and approved the final wording.

## Core Questions

- What should a new PM produce in the first 48 hours to support the next version meeting?
- How should staffing loss, post-freeze requests, and excess testing scope become evidence, cost, risk, and opportunity-cost decisions?
- How can results, user segments, production process, and team state lead to a practical three-month roadmap?

## Process

I mapped the project across IP expression, action PVP, live operations, and community response. The SOP then produces a project brief, experience map, user segments, requirement-owner table, top risks, and questions for the next version meeting. Each conflict follows the same logic: confirm the goal, assess evidence, estimate impact, propose graded options, and identify the decision owner.

## AI Collaboration

GPT was used to explore structure and alternatives, while Codex helped organize the final material and appendix. AI may summarize information or suggest risks, but it does not decide who the version serves, what to protect, or when to escalate. The Codex risk-monitoring section is a proposed workflow, not a production deployment.

## Outcomes

The final submission covers the first 48 hours, three conflict decisions, meeting actions, launch review, and a three-month roadmap, and received an S grade. The grade reflects assignment quality; it does not imply that the simulated workflow has been validated in a commercial production team.

## Retrospective

The study helped translate my gameplay, rapid-development, and team-delivery experience into PM artifacts. Technical background helps identify dependencies, test scope, and implementation risk earlier, but project management still depends on clear goals, ownership, and escalation paths.
